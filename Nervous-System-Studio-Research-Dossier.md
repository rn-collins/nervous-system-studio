# Nervous System Studio — Research Dossier

**A primary-sourced, evidence-graded foundation for the platform's theory, method, and product language.**

Version 1.0 · Compiled July 6, 2026 · Internal + public-facing reference

---

## How to read this document

This dossier underpins everything Nervous System Studio claims and does. It exists to keep the product **honest**: to make sure the theory is defensible, the method is sound, the copy is calibrated to the actual strength of the evidence, and we never drift into medical, diagnostic, or overstated territory.

Every load-bearing claim is graded:

- **STRONG** — multiple RCTs, large cohorts, or converging meta-analyses; well replicated.
- **MODERATE** — real signal, but with meaningful limits (small samples, confounding, heterogeneity, or modest effect sizes).
- **PRELIMINARY / CONTESTED** — small, single-lab, correlational, advocacy-driven, or failing replication. Interesting, not settled.

A companion section — **"What the evidence does NOT support"** — is treated as equally important. Knowing the boundary of the evidence is what separates a credible tool from a wellness gimmick.

> **Standing constraint:** Nervous System Studio is a self-reflection tool. Nothing in this dossier is used to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any condition, to recommend cannabis for any purpose, or to make personal health predictions. Population-level research findings are never presented to a user as a fact about their own body.

## 1. The theoretical framework

Nervous System Studio rests on four evidence-backed pillars. Together they justify a set-and-setting personalization tool without requiring any medical claim.

### Pillar 1 — Environments measurably shape state
Specific, controllable features of an environment — especially **light, sound, and temperature** — have demonstrable effects on arousal, mood, cognition, and sleep. The strongest, most mechanistic effects are **light on the circadian/alerting system** and **noise on cognition and sleep**. (See §2, §6.)

### Pillar 2 — People differ in how strongly environments affect them
**Environmental Sensitivity** research (Pluess) and **Sensory Processing Sensitivity** (Aron) describe stable individual differences in how deeply people process and react to their surroundings. Critically, the **vantage sensitivity** finding suggests more-sensitive individuals may *benefit more* from supportive environments — a genuine mechanistic rationale for personalization. (See §3.) We treat sensitivity as a **spectrum tendency**, never a label or diagnosis, because the construct's discriminant validity is contested.

### Pillar 3 — "Set and setting" is a real, if bounded, lens
The idea that experience is shaped by the substance, the person's mindset ("set"), and the environment ("setting") is a well-established framework (Zinberg, 1984) that overlaps with modern **expectancy/placebo** science. Expectancy effects on subjective experience are strongest in the alcohol literature and **moderate/mixed for cannabis** — so we frame set-and-setting descriptively and reflectively, never as a way to control or guarantee an outcome. (See §5.)

### Pillar 4 — Within-person self-tracking can produce valid personal insight
**In-the-moment logging** (ecological momentary assessment) reduces recall bias, and **well-designed within-person (N-of-1) experiments** can yield internally valid, individualized evidence — *for that person*. This is the methodological engine of the product, and it comes with strict cautions (reactivity, confounding, correlation ≠ causation, regression to the mean). (See §4.)

**The synthesis:** environments demonstrably affect state (P1); some people more than others (P2); mindset and context modulate experience (P3); and a disciplined within-person method can surface which patterns are actually *yours* (P4) — as hypotheses to test, not conclusions.

## 2. Environmental & sensory psychology

### Lighting
- **Room light (<200 lux) before bed suppresses and shortens melatonin in ~99% of people (~90 min shorter duration).** Gooley et al., 2011, *J. Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism* (within-subject lab, n=116). **STRONG.**
- **Cool/blue-enriched evening light (~6500K) suppresses melatonin and raises alertness more than warm (~2700K) light.** Chellappa et al., 2011, *PLoS ONE* (RCT crossover). **STRONG** (melatonin) / **MODERATE** (cognition). No benefit on executive tasks; small all-male sample.
- **Daytime bright light improves alertness and some cognition — but not reliably mood.** Siraji et al., 2023, *LEUKOS* (meta-analysis); Souman/Lok et al., 2021, *Frontiers in Psychology* (review). **MODERATE.**

### Noise / soundscape
- **Chronic ambient noise impairs children's cognition** (pooled SMD ≈ −0.54). 2025 meta-analysis, *Acoustics*; Thompson et al., 2022, *Environment International*. **STRONG.**
- **Nighttime noise disturbs sleep** with robust exposure–response curves. Basner & McGuire, 2018, *IJERPH* (WHO review). **STRONG** (sleep disturbance); CVD link **MODERATE**.
- **Music listening reduces anxiety and stress markers** (medium-to-large effects in clinical contexts). de Witte et al., systematic review + meta-analysis; *eClinicalMedicine*, 2025. **STRONG.**
- **Nature sounds aid stress recovery vs. urban noise** — modest and partial. Alvarsson et al., 2010, *IJERPH* (skin-conductance recovery; HRV null in same study). **MODERATE / PRELIMINARY.**

### Visual clutter / disorder
- **A "cluttered/stressful home" correlated with a flatter (adverse) diurnal cortisol slope in women.** Saxbe & Repetti, 2010, *PSPB* (n=60). **MODERATE, correlational only — no causal claim.**
- **Visual clutter competes for limited attentional resources** (biased-competition neuroscience). McMains & Kastner, 2011, *J. Neuroscience*. **MODERATE (lab/mechanistic).** Extrapolation to "a messy desk lowers productivity" is inference, not demonstration.

### Scent / olfaction
- **Inhaled lavender aromatherapy for anxiety: promising but methodologically weak; expectancy is a real confound.** Multiple low-quality meta-analyses. **PRELIMINARY / CONTESTED.** (The strong anxiolytic data are for *oral* Silexan — a different route — and do NOT validate diffusing scent in a room.)
- **Odor–mood effects are heavily shaped by learned association and labeling** (Herz). **MODERATE.** Generalized "scent X → state Y" claims are unreliable across individuals.

### Color
- **Beyond light spectrum's circadian effect, there is little robust evidence that wall/object color reliably changes mood, stress, or productivity.** Mehta & Zhu, 2009, *Science* (small, task-specific, inconsistently replicated). **PRELIMINARY / CONTESTED.**

### Temperature
- **Cognitive performance declines at high temperatures; the precise inverted-U curve is contested.** Porras-Salazar et al., 2021, *Building and Environment* (35-study meta-analysis; peak ~21.6°C). A 2024 meta-analysis found no reliable relationship within 20–30°C. **MODERATE.** Comfort and performance can diverge.

## 3. Nervous system, sensitivity & the mechanistic backbone

- **The autonomic nervous system regulates arousal via sympathetic ("mobilize") and parasympathetic/vagal ("restore") branches; vagally-mediated HRV (RMSSD, HF-HRV) indexes the vagal brake.** Shaffer & Ginsburg, 2017. **STRONG** (concept). SDNN/LF do not cleanly index one branch.
- **Acute stress reliably lowers HRV.** Kim et al., 2018, *Psychiatry Investigation* (meta-analysis). **STRONG** at the group level; a single reading tells an individual little.
- **Chronic stress produces cumulative "wear and tear" — allostatic load.** McEwen & Stellar, 1993; Juster et al., 2010. **STRONG as framework** / MODERATE for any single biomarker.
- **Interoception splits into dissociable dimensions (accuracy, sensibility, awareness) that often don't correlate; body-attention is double-edged and can amplify anxiety.** Garfinkel et al., 2015, *Biological Psychology*; 2024 meta-analysis on interoception & anxiety. **STRONG** (measurement model) / **MODERATE** (anxiety link).
- **Sensory Processing Sensitivity is a measurable individual difference, but the HSP scale is not unidimensional and overlaps substantially with neuroticism/introversion.** Aron & Aron, 1997; Smolewska et al., 2006. **MODERATE / CONTESTED.**
- **Sensitivity is a continuum (roughly low/medium/high groups), not a binary type; the popular "15–20% are HSPs" is a soft estimate.** Lionetti et al., 2018, *Translational Psychiatry* (latent-class). **MODERATE** (continuum).
- **Vantage sensitivity: more-sensitive individuals may benefit disproportionately from supportive environments/interventions.** Pluess & Belsky, 2013; de Villiers et al., 2018. **MODERATE.** This is the core rationale for personalization — presented as a promising hypothesis.

**Contested / easy to overclaim:** HRV as a personal "stress score" (noisy, metric-dependent); cortisol metrics (CAR, diurnal slope have poor individual test–retest reliability — 2023 meta-analysis); HSP as a fixed identity; differential-susceptibility GxE replication; "just tune into your body" (can backfire for anxious users). **No diagnosis, ever** — sensory overload ≠ autism/ADHD; allostatic load ≠ a disease state.

## 4. The method — self-tracking, EMA & N-of-1

- **Monitoring progress toward a goal reliably improves attainment** (d+ = 0.40; larger when recorded). Harkin et al., 2016, *Psychological Bulletin* (meta-analysis, 138 studies, N≈19,951). **STRONG.**
- **In-the-moment logging (EMA) reduces recall bias vs. retrospective reports, especially for variable experiences.** Shiffman et al., 2008, *Annual Review of Clinical Psychology*; Stone et al., 2005. **STRONG.**
- **Well-designed within-person (N-of-1) experiments yield internally valid individual evidence — when they include real, repeated, ideally randomized manipulation (≥3 demonstrations).** WWC Single-Case Design Standards; *Translational Psychiatry*, 2023 (review). **STRONG as method.** Passive observation does *not* inherit this causal warrant.
- **Expressive writing / journaling produces small average benefits that are inconsistent and often null in rigorous RCT-only syntheses.** Frattaroli, 2006; Pennebaker, 2018. **PRELIMINARY / CONTESTED** for reliable benefit.
- **The act of measuring itself nudges behavior (reactivity), but the effect is small and likely inflated by bias.** Question-behavior-effect meta-analyses (SMD ≈ 0.06–0.09). **MODERATE (real, small).**

**Documented pitfalls we must design against:** recall bias, self-report/social-desirability bias, **reactivity** (the app's own logs are partly an artifact of using it — disclose this), confounding, **correlation ≠ causation**, **regression to the mean** (extreme days rebound on their own), small-sample/short-series noise, and **multiple comparisons** (tracking many variables guarantees false "findings").

### Product-language principles (derived directly from §4)
Scale the strength of the claim to the strength of the evidence, using a tiered lexicon:

1. **Association (enough data):** "On days you logged X, your Y tended to be higher." / "X **appears associated with** Y in your logs."
2. **Hypothesis (invitation to act):** "a **pattern to explore**," "**consider testing** whether…"
3. **Personal causal (only after a within-person test):** "In your test, when you tried X on several occasions, Y tended to…"

**Never, by default:** "causes," "proven," "will," "because of," "fixes," "guarantees," or any clinical/diagnostic verb. Always: disclose that these are self-reports; require a minimum data threshold before surfacing a pattern; show tentativeness ("based on 6 entries — treat as preliminary"); avoid crediting the tool for natural rebounds; and preserve the user's authority ("Does this ring true for you?").

## 5. Cannabis, set & setting (non-medical)

- **"Set and setting" was systematized by Zinberg (1984); in modern psychopharmacology it overlaps strongly with expectancy/placebo theory.** Zinberg, 1984; Hartogsohn, 2016/2017. **STRONG** (as framework) / **MODERATE** (synthesis). It is a heuristic, not a validated causal model.
- **Expectancy independently shifts subjective drug experience — clearest in alcohol balanced-placebo studies; real but less consistent for cannabis.** Marlatt et al., 1973 (alcohol, **STRONG**, by analogy); Metrik et al., 2009/2012 (cannabis, **MODERATE**).
- **THC shows a biphasic, dose-dependent effect on anxiety in controlled settings (lower dose reduced stress reactivity; higher dose increased anxiety).** Childs et al., 2017, *Drug & Alcohol Dependence* (RCT, n=42, oral). **MODERATE** — small, oral, healthy volunteers; **not dosing guidance**.
- **The largest meta-analysis to date did NOT find contextual factors (environment, social group, expectancy, time) to be significant predictors of subjective cannabis effects** — though it cautions the underlying literature is weak. 2024, *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* (29 studies). **MODERATE.** This directly tempers any strong "setting shapes the high" claim.
- **Acute anxiety/panic is the most common acute adverse reaction; risk is elevated by identifiable factors** (naïve use, higher dose, high-THC/low-CBD, prior adverse reaction, pre-existing anxiety). Sharpe et al., 2020. **MODERATE.**

**Claims we must NOT make:** that cannabis or any setting treats/prevents/relieves any condition; any dose or product recommendation; any safety guarantee; that optimizing set-and-setting prevents a bad reaction; that we help anyone obtain or choose cannabis. **Defensible framing:** the historical set/setting framework + general expectancy science + wide individual variation — as prompts for self-reflection and personal journaling, never advice.

## 6. Sleep & evening environment

- **The circadian system is highly light-sensitive; ~100 lux gives roughly half-maximal melatonin suppression, and sensitivity varies >50-fold between people.** Zeitzer et al., 2000, *J. Physiology*; Phillips et al., 2019, *PNAS*. **STRONG** (dose-response) / **STRONG** (variability). → personalize, don't prescribe a universal number.
- **An expert consensus recommends ≤10 lux melanopic EDI in the evening and <1 lux during sleep (healthy adults).** Brown et al., 2022, *PLoS Biology* (consensus). **MODERATE** (recommendation-level).
- **Light during sleep (~100 lux) worsens overnight cardiometabolic markers via a sympathetic pathway** (melatonin not suppressed). Mason et al., 2022, *PNAS* (RCT crossover, n=20). **MODERATE** (small, acute surrogates).
- **A cool, quiet, dark bedroom supports sleep** (heat above the thermoneutral zone and noise fragment sleep; ~<35 dB and ~20–25°C commonly cited). Okamoto-Mizuno & Mizuno, 2012; WHO noise guidelines. **MODERATE–STRONG.**
- **Regularity of sleep/wake timing predicts mortality better than duration.** Windred et al., 2024, *SLEEP* (UK Biobank, n≈61,000, accelerometer). **MODERATE–STRONG** (large, prospective, observational).
- **Screens before bed are associated with later/poorer sleep, but effects are modest and driven more by timing/arousal than blue light; blue-blocking interventions are inconsistent.** 2025 meta-analysis, *Frontiers in Psychiatry*; Chang et al., 2015, *PNAS*. **PRELIMINARY / CONTESTED** for a large causal blue-light effect.
- **Sleep hygiene is supportive/preventive, NOT a treatment for chronic insomnia** (CBT-I is first-line). Irish et al., 2015; AASM guidance (Edinger et al., 2021). **STRONG** (consensus). → the app optimizes environments; it does not treat sleep disorders.

## 7. What the evidence does NOT support (guardrails)

- Wall/paint **color** reliably changing mood or focus (circadian effects come from *light spectrum*, not decor color).
- "A messy desk makes you more creative" (Vohs 2013 creativity effect failed to replicate) — or that clutter **causes** elevated cortisol (one small correlational, women-only study).
- **Diffusing lavender** working like an anxiety medication (inhaled evidence is weak/expectancy-confounded).
- **Blue-light-blocking glasses** reliably fixing sleep (meta-analyses inconsistent).
- Any **personal reading of HRV or cortisol** as a "stress score" (noisy, unreliable at the individual level).
- **HSP as a diagnosis or fixed type**, or a hard "20% of people" figure.
- That **environment reliably controls the cannabis experience** (largest meta-analysis found no significant contextual effect).
- Any claim that the app **treats, cures, prevents, or diagnoses** anything, or that it changes your nervous system or induces any physiological process.

General theme: most environmental effects are **modest, context-dependent, and often lab-only**; the safest, best-supported levers are **light timing/intensity, a dark-cool-quiet sleep space, consistent sleep timing, and music for calm**. Everything else is offered as a personal pattern to explore.

## 8. How the evidence maps into the product

| Product element | Grounded in | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Sensory Profile quiz** | Environmental Sensitivity spectrum (§3) | Frames sensitivity as a tendency on a spectrum, never a label or diagnosis. |
| **In-the-moment logging** | EMA reduces recall bias (§4) | Encourages logging close to the moment; discloses that logs are self-reports. |
| **Insight engine** | Association-not-causation, min-n, multiple comparisons, regression to mean (§4) | Tiered language; requires a data threshold; caps simultaneous insights; shows tentativeness; never claims causation. |
| **"Next experiment" (N-of-1)** | Single-case methodology (§4) | Reframed as a real within-person test: change one input, repeat several times, compare — the one path to stronger, personal language. |
| **Blueprints** | §2, §6 evidence strength | Lead with strong levers (evening light, dark/cool/quiet, regularity, music); soften weak ones (color, scent-as-medicine, clutter-causal). Each element carries an honest confidence framing. |
| **Cannabis context** | §5 | Optional, descriptive, non-medical; no dosing, no purchasing, no guarantees; acknowledges evidence is mixed. |
| **Safety posture** | §3, §5, §6 guardrails | No diagnosis, no treatment claims, no personal biomarker readings; directs persistent concerns to professionals. |
